---
"idemfence": minor
---

Add the lease heartbeat, `ctx.signal` and `maxHold`. In lease mode the lease is now renewed every `lease / 3` while the handler runs, on the store's own connections, each renewal starting only after the previous one finished. The heartbeat stops (and any renewal in flight finishes) before the response is captured, completed or released. A route can turn it off with `heartbeat: false` (validated with `ROUTE_FLAG_INVALID`) and renew with `ctx.renew()` instead. Every handler context now has `ctx.signal`. In lease mode it aborts when a renewal (heartbeat or `ctx.renew()`) is fenced, with an `IdemfenceFencedError` as the reason; the handler is not otherwise interrupted, and its later complete is fenced as before. It also aborts when the lease has been held for `maxHold`, with the new `IdemfenceHoldLimitError` (code `HOLD_LIMIT`). In transactional mode, on fail-open runs and on requests idemfence does not apply to, it never aborts, and it is never the request's signal: a client disconnect does not abort it. The new instance option `maxHold` (default `min(10 × lease, retention)`; it must be greater than `lease` and no greater than `retention`, validated with `MAX_HOLD_NOT_LONGER_THAN_LEASE` and `MAX_HOLD_LONGER_THAN_RETENTION`) bounds how long one process keeps a lease alive. It is measured on the process's monotonic clock; after it, the heartbeat stops, `ctx.renew()` rejects with `IdemfenceHoldLimitError`, and the lease expires on the store's clock, so a key is held at most `maxHold + lease`. A failed renewal logs `renew-failed` and is retried at the next interval; fenced renewals and the hold limit log `fenced` and `hold-limit`. New exports: `IdemfenceHoldLimitError` and the `HandlerSignal` type.
